    title: "A tangent developable"
    caption: |
      A surface in $\RR^3$ with gaussian curvature zero is called a "developable surface", because it can be rolled out of a sheet of paper. The gaussian curvature is the product of principle curvatures, so if the gaussian curvature is zero, there must be a direction with zero normal curvature through every point. Following these directions along a surface traces out a line in three dimensional space. Therefore, any surface with gaussian curvature zero must be swept out by lines in $\RR^3$. These are called ruled surfaces, because you can lie a ruler along the surface.
      
      There are three classes of developable surfaces, classified by their lines of ruling. 
      <ul>
      <li>Planes: These have lines of ruling in any direction</li>
      <li>Cones: These have lines of ruling which meet at a point. This point may be at infinity, producing a cylinder</li>
      <li>Tangent developables: The lines of ruling are all tangent to a curve in $\RR^3$</li>
      </ul>
      
      The third class is most mysterious, and I've been searching for a curved origami piece which shows off tangent developables. The picture above is my attempt. I started with a huge sheet of pulped cardboard, the thinnest cutting mat I could find. Then, I folded it in half, and cut a curve out of both. This will become the inside spine of the final sculpture. Then, I taped the 
      <div class="text-center"> <img src="/assets/crafts/tori/tangent_template.jpeg" alt="a lune out of paper. There are two layers, taped along a central curve. " width="400" > </div>
      
      The torus is constructed out of two spirals, one green and one red, one rotating clockwise and the other counterclockwise. These weave between one another, interfacing using the provided notches. 
      
      
      I kept adding layers until I could comfertably wrap the stack around into a circle. It took about 50 layers of each color. Here's what the accordian looks like all compressed.
      <div class="text-center"> <img src="/assets/crafts/tori/compressed.jpeg" alt="thick stack of circular paper, about 4 inches thick " width="400" > </div>
      
      The final torus is fun to hold because it rotates around its axis very well. If you just grab the outside, it can wriggle from your grasp like one of those water tube toys.
